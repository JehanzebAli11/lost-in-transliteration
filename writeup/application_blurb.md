## Project summary (150 words)

I built *Lost in Transliteration*, a reproducible study of prompt-injection defences in English, Urdu script, Roman Urdu and code-switched Urdu-English. I tested 400 attacks translated by an open-weight model (I checked 60 translations myself: meaning kept in all 60) and 57 written natively in Urdu variants by another model. <!-- results/translation_report.json; results/human_review_summary.csv; results/native_gate_report.json --> Most classifiers lost recall outside English: Meta's Prompt Guard 2 fell from 48.5% to 11.5% on Roman Urdu (paired difference −37 points, 95% CI −42 to −32). ProtectAI's flagged 85% of benign Urdu-script text. <!-- results/table_detection_drop.csv: prompt-guard-2 roman_ur; protectai-v2 fpr ur 0.847 --> Across five models at one output cap (sampling settings differ), one of 15 language comparisons survived correction: Claude Haiku 5.5 followed Urdu-script injections more often (52 vs 31 of 150). <!-- results/table_asr_gap.csv setting same, p_holm 0.011; results/rq3_settings.csv --> The fine-tuned detector my selection rule (fixed before training) picked caught 14 of 57 native attacks; one chosen afterwards caught 52 (one seed). <!-- results/headline_numbers.json best_detector; results/detector_summary.csv --> All data is model-written.

## What I would research as a Resident (250 words)

This project left me with a question that outputs alone cannot answer. For four classifiers, translated attacks still ranked above benign text (ROC-AUC 0.98 or higher), yet fell below the decision threshold. <!-- results/fig3_roc_auc.csv --> In one example, "ignore" and "system prompt" survived in a Roman Urdu attack, and Prompt Guard 2's score still fell from 1.00 to 0.03. <!-- results/qualitative_failures.md atk-0090 --> My hypothesis is that script and surrounding grammar shift the decision more than they change whether a model represents the text as an instruction. I measured only scores, so I cannot tell; I want to find out, in small classifiers and large models.

As a Resident I would work on three linked problems. First, I would measure where in a model's activations an injection becomes detectable, and whether that signal transfers across scripts: Urdu, Roman Urdu, Hindi and Hinglish, Arabic and Arabizi. A linear probe was the stretch goal I did not reach here. Second, I would test defences that use that structure, such as per-language calibration or transliteration before classification, against human-written attacks, with several seeds and selection rules fixed in advance. My own detector showed why: the pick I fixed in advance fit the templates and missed most native attacks. Third, I would study agents: tool descriptions and retrieved documents arrive in whatever language third parties write, and the rule-based MCP scanners I tested missed subtle poisoning even in English. <!-- results/mcp_summary.csv: rule-based scanners en 0/10 -->

I am a native Urdu speaker, and I want safety tools to work for people who write the way many Pakistanis do online. <!-- OWNER: personal line drafted by the agent; PLAN §3.5 says only "native Urdu speaker". Edit to your own words. -->
